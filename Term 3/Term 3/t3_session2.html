3. Three Things Have To Change

Dropping in a bigger file is only the first of them. The other two are forced by it.

3.1  The box of pieces is too small

Your tokenizer glues characters together until it has VOCAB_SIZE pieces. Four physics topics needed 4,096. Twenty subjects bring chlorophyll, mitochondria and voltage — and at 4,096 there is no room to learn any of them, so they get shattered into scraps.

Why shattering hurts

A word split into seven scraps has to be reassembled from seven separate guesses, and every one of them is a chance to get it wrong. Worse, the model cannot build a single clean meaning for photosynthesis — it only ever sees ph, ot, os and the rest, each of which also turns up in a hundred unrelated words.

3.2  Nobody can wait an hour and a half

Here is the problem that bites first. Your data_pipeline.py learns the glue rules every time you run it. On a 36 MB book that was slow. On a 190 MB book it is impossible.

Before you start: what are tokenizer.json and tokens.pt?

Activity 1 hands you two files you have never seen before. They are not new ideas — they are the two slow jobs from section 3.2, already done once and saved.

 tokenizer.jsontokens.pt

Why two files and not one? Because they answer different questions. The rulebook says how to cut any text into pieces — including a question you type tomorrow that appears nowhere in the book. The numbered book is only this one book, already cut up. You cannot work the rules back out from the numbers, so you need both.

What each one is for:

  • tokens.pt is what training reads. The training loop never sees words at all — only numbers, and this is where they come from.
  • tokenizer.json is what talking needs. Without it you could still train, but you would have no way to turn "what is an acid?" into numbers, or turn the model's answer back into English.

Neither file is a secret you are being kept from. Delete both and your code still works — it simply rebuilds them, and you wait the 110 minutes. That is exactly what the if os.path.exists(...) checks in Activity 1 are for.

    def save(self, path):
        """Write the learned rules out, so training happens once."""
        with open(path, "w", encoding="utf-8") as f:
            json.dump({
                "pieces": [self.itos[i] for i in range(len(self.itos))],
                "merges": [list(pair) for pair in self.merges],
            }, f)

    def load(self, path):
        """Read the rules back. Gives exactly the tokenizer that saved them."""
        with open(path, encoding="utf-8") as f:
            saved = json.load(f)
        pieces = saved["pieces"]
        self.stoi = {p: i for i, p in enumerate(pieces)}
        self.itos = {i: p for i, p in enumerate(pieces)}
        self.merges = [tuple(pair) for pair in saved["merges"]]
        self.cache = {}

7. It Knows More Than It Says

Those broken words are not the model being stupid. We are about to prove that — and then fix them in fifteen lines, with no retraining at all.

Your model never picks a word

This surprises almost everyone. It does not choose. It produces a chance for all 6,144 pieces at once, and then your code rolls a weighted dice over every single one of them. The choosing is not the model's job — it is your job, and so far you have been doing it in the laziest possible way.

"Photosynthesis is how plants grow…", "…how plants produce…", "…how plants convert…". Any of them makes a sensible sentence. The model knows exactly what it is doing. So where does askngine come from?

7.2  Where the broken words come from

From everything below those eight. Here is the model's full 100% of confidence, split into three parts:

68.5%
20.2%
11.3%
the 12 best pieces — all sensible words ranks 13–40 — still reasonable 6,104 hopeless pieces — nonsense

Your code rolls a dice over all three parts

Each hopeless piece on its own is nearly impossible — about 0.0019%. But there are 6,104 of them, and added together they hold 11.3% of the chance.

So roughly 1 pick in 9, your model reaches straight past every sensible word and grabs junk. Land one of those in the middle of a word and engine becomes askngine. The model was never confused. The dice were.

7.3  The four dials — and what each one does to that red band

Every dial has the same job: shrink or delete the red band before the dice is rolled. Each one goes about it differently.

Temperature = 0.6

How bold is it?

Divides every score before they become percentages. Below 1.0 makes the favourite even more favoured, which squeezes everything weak downwards.

before11.3%
after0.68%

Top pick also rises 18.4% → 39.8%. It becomes more decisive.

Top-k = 40

Only the best few are allowed

Rank every piece, keep the best 40, delete the other 6,104 outright — no matter what their chances were.

before11.3%
after0%

Blunt, and it removes the entire red band in one move.

Top-p = 0.92

Keep going until you have enough

Go down the ranked list adding percentages. Stop once the running total passes 92%. Cut everything below. It adapts to how sure the model is.

surekeeps 3
tornkeeps 10

Running after the other dials: at the first word it kept 3. Mid-sentence, where the model is far less sure, it kept 10.

Repetition penalty = 1.25

Stop saying the same thing

Pushes down any piece already used in the last 40. Nothing to do with the red band — this one fixes a different bug.

before"such as oil… such as oil and slows"
after"Lubricants such as grease reduce…"

Kills the loops a model falls into inside one answer.

Every bar below is measured from qa_model_v5.pth, at the moment it had just written "Photosynthesis is how plants" and had to choose the next piece.

How to read this

  • Each row is one possible next piece — a word, or a chunk of one.
  • The bar length is its chance of being chosen. Longer bar = the model thinks it fits better. The number on the right is that chance as a percentage.
  • All the bars add up to 100%. The model always spreads exactly one whole share of confidence across all 6,144 pieces.
  • The bottom three bars are groups. There are 6,144 pieces in total and we cannot draw them all, so the weaker ones are bundled: the orange bars are ranks 13–31 and 32–40 added together, and the red bar is all 6,104 hopeless ones added together.
  • Greyed out and struck through = deleted. A dial has removed that option; it now has zero chance of being picked.

Click a dial and watch the bars move. Nothing about the model changes — only which options survive to be picked from. Each button shows that one dial on its own. In the real code all four run one after another — that is section 7.5.

These are your model’s genuine numbers. Only the dial changes between views — the underlying scores are the same every time.

The one and only place your AI is random

All four dials run in order, then one piece is drawn at random from whatever survived. That single line is the only randomness in your whole project. The tokenizer, the embedding, the attention heads — every one of those gives the identical answer every time. Asking the same question twice gives two answers only because of that one dice roll.

🛠 Activity 5: Fix the picking

No retraining. You are changing how the model chooses, not what it knows.

Step 1: Add the four dials

  • Open attention_engine.py and find class PredictionEngine.
  • Scroll to the end of its forward method — the line return logits.
  • Directly under that line, still inside the class, add the settings and the new method:
attention_engine.py — add UNDER "return logits", inside PredictionEngine
        logits = self.lm_head(x)  # turn it back into a guess
        return logits

    # The four dials. They live on the class so that every file using
    # this model picks pieces the same way.
    TEMPERATURE = 0.6
    TOP_K = 40
    TOP_P = 0.92
    REPETITION_PENALTY = 1.25
    REPETITION_WINDOW = 40

    def sample_next(self, logits, recent_ids=None):
        """Pick ONE piece: repetition penalty, temperature, top-k, top-p."""

        # 1. push down anything used in the last 40 pieces
        if recent_ids:
            for tid in set(recent_ids):
                if logits[0, tid] < 0:
                    logits[0, tid] *= self.REPETITION_PENALTY
                else:
                    logits[0, tid] /= self.REPETITION_PENALTY

        # 2. temperature: below 1.0 makes the favourite even more favoured
        logits = logits / max(self.TEMPERATURE, 1e-5)

        # 3. top-k: keep the best 40, delete the other 6,104
        if self.TOP_K > 0:
            v, _ = torch.topk(logits, min(self.TOP_K, logits.size(-1)))
            logits[logits < v[:, [-1]]] = -float('inf')

        # 4. top-p: keep only enough pieces to cover 92% of the chance
        if self.TOP_P < 1.0:
            sorted_logits, sorted_idx = torch.sort(logits, descending=True)
            cumulative = torch.cumsum(F.softmax(sorted_logits, dim=-1), dim=-1)
            remove = cumulative > self.TOP_P
            remove[..., 1:] = remove[..., :-1].clone()
            remove[..., 0] = False
            logits[remove.scatter(1, sorted_idx, remove)] = -float('inf')

        return torch.multinomial(F.softmax(logits, dim=-1), num_samples=1)

Why you need all four, not the best one

Each does a job the others cannot:

  • Repetition penalty fixes loops. Nothing else in the list even looks at what was already said.
  • Temperature reshapes every chance at once. It deletes nothing — it makes the good options stand further above the bad ones, so the cuts that follow land better.
  • Top-k is a hard ceiling. No matter how lost the model gets, it can never choose from more than 40 pieces.
  • Top-p is the adaptive one, and it does the real work: 7 options when the model is torn, 1 when it is sure.

Top-k alone would leave 40 options open in both cases above — far too many when the answer is obviously "Photosynthesis". Top-p alone, without temperature first, would keep 54. Together they land on 10 and 3. That is the difference between a model that guesses and one that answers.

Standard Task — what to expect

Somewhere between 0.9 and 1.0 the made-up words return, because above that the long shots get enough probability to be picked. Going the other way costs you something different: at 0.3 the writing is clean but the same question gives nearly the same answer every time, and the model starts looping on safe phrases. Low temperature is not "more correct", it is more timid. There is no single best value — you are trading variety against reliability, and 0.6 is where this model sits comfortably.

Advanced Task — what you should find

Top-k does most of the work on broken words, because it deletes the 6,104 worst options outright and invented pieces live almost entirely in that tail. Temperature helps by shrinking the tail from 18.4% to 1.85% before top-k even looks at it. Top-p matters most when the model is confident — at the first word of a known answer it cut 40 down to 6. The repetition penalty fixes a different problem and will barely change your invented-word count; to see its effect, generate 300 tokens instead of 110 and watch for looping.

Engineering Takeaway

You changed four things at once and it worked. That feels good and tells you nothing. Changing one thing at a time is slower and is the only way to learn which thing mattered.

Did You Know?

Those four dials are not a teaching simplification — they are the real controls. Every commercial model API exposes temperature and top_p to developers, and the defaults sit close to the numbers you just used. When someone complains that an AI "got more creative and started making things up", they are usually describing a temperature set too high. You now know exactly what that means, because you watched it happen on your own model.

⚙ Troubleshooting & FAQs
❓ RuntimeError: size mismatch … loading qa_model_v4.pth

Session 1's model was built for 4,096 pieces. Today's engine builds itself for 6,144, so the two word tables are different shapes and the old weights will never fit. Load qa_model_v5.pth instead. To run the old model again you need its old tokenizer too.

❓ My run says "No saved tokenizer. Learning the rules"

tokenizer.json did not upload, or landed in a different folder from data_pipeline.py. Stop the cell — otherwise you will wait about ninety minutes. Check the file is there and is around 154 KB, then run again.

❓ Training started but the loss is stuck above 5

Almost always tokens.pt arriving incomplete. Check its size: it should be about 84 MB. A partial upload loads without error and gives the model nonsense to read.

❓ The answers are still full of broken words after I added the dials

Check that generate() actually calls sample_next(). It is easy to add the four constants and the new method and then forget to replace the old torch.multinomial line inside the loop — in which case nothing you added is ever used.

❓ Every answer is now almost identical

Your TEMPERATURE is too low, or TOP_K is very small. Both narrow the field; together they can narrow it to one. Put temperature back to 0.6 and top-k to 40, then change only one at a time.
