0. Where We Are

A word you need first: parameter

A parameter is one number inside your model. Your model holds millions of them.

Before training, every parameter is a random number, so the model's guesses are random too. Training makes a guess, checks how wrong it was, and nudges every parameter a tiny bit. After millions of nudges, these numbers hold everything the model knows.

Think of each parameter as one tiny dial. Training turns millions of dials, a little at a time, until the model's answers sound right. More dials means more room to store what it learns.

We write big counts with an M, which means million. So 7.9M means 7.9 million (about 7,900,000).

1. Why A Wider Notepad

In one sentence Every token gets a notepad with 256 lines. Notes for twenty subjects do not fit, so they get written over each other. Today you give every token 512 lines.

Back to the study group

Remember the study group from Session 3. Your question is a row of students. Each student holds one token and has a notepad. The notepad has 256 lines (n_embd = 256).

Everything the model knows about that token must fit on those lines. That includes what the token means, which of the twenty subjects it belongs to, and which word usually comes next.

Your book now has twenty subjects. On 256 lines, the notes for different subjects start to land on the same lines. They squash on top of each other, so the model mixes up its facts. Today you give every student 512 lines.

Two reminders from Session 3

n_embd. The setting at the top of attention_engine.py that says how many numbers describe each token. That is the number of lines on the notepad. People also call it the model's width. A wider model is a study group with bigger notepads.

2. Where The Extra Numbers Go

In one sentence Doubling the width does not double the model. It makes it about three times bigger, because most of the model is made of squares, and a square twice as wide holds four times as many numbers.

Double the width, but more than double the model

You double n_embd, from 256 to 512. You might expect the model to double too. It does not. It grows 3.2 times, from exactly 7,951,872 to exactly 25,334,784 parameters.

The reason is the shape of the layers inside. Most of them are squares of numbers: n_embd rows × n_embd columns. In v7 that is 256 × 256 = 65,536 numbers. When you make a square twice as wide, it also gets twice as tall. So it holds four times as many numbers.

Think of a rug. A rug 2 metres by 2 metres covers 4 square metres. Make it twice as big on both sides, 4 metres by 4 metres, and it covers 16 square metres. That is four times the rug, not twice.

One reminder: layer

A layer is one round of look-and-think on the notepads. Your model has 6 layers, in v7 and in v8. Today changes how wide each layer is, not how many there are.

  2. In v8, n_embd = 512. The square is now 512 wide and 512 tall. Both sides grow, not just one.
  3. 512 is two lots of 256. So the big square is exactly four small squares: two across and two down.
  4. Four small squares hold 4 × 65,536 = 262,144 numbers. Double the width, and you get four times the numbers.

4. v7 Against v8: Judge It Yourself

In one sentence A bigger model is better on average, not on every question. So you judge it with a checklist and a big test, never with one answer.

The scorecard: six checks for every answer

A good answer passes all six checks. Use them for every answer you read today.

  1. Stays on the question (it answers what you asked, not a nearby question).
  2. The right fact (the science is correct).
  3. Real words only (no made-up words).
  4. Sentences make sense (no gibberish, no sentence that says two opposite things).
  5. Finishes its sentences (no sentence that stops halfway).
  6. Right words for the subject (it uses gravity words for gravity, cell words for cells).

Why does the model make up words?

Your model only knows the words in its book. When you ask about a word the book rarely or never uses, the model has nothing to copy. So it glues together pieces it does know, and it builds a word that sounds right but is not real.

We counted how often each word appears in your book. The panel shows what the tokenizer does with each one.

Words the book knows, and words it does not
mitochondriamitochondria761 times in the book
biomoleculebiomolecule0 times in the book
v8 wrote: BiomixisBiomixis0 times: not a word

The tokenizer stores "mitochondria" as one whole piece, and the book uses it 761 times. v8 answers that question correctly. The book never uses "biomolecule", so v8 glues known pieces into "Biomixis".

A second trap: two words that start the same way

You ask v8 "what is a neuron". It answers: "Neutrons are like tiny messengers..." The book's real answer says: "Neurons are like tiny messengers in your brain..."

So v8 remembered the right idea, but it wrote the wrong word. Here is why, one step at a time.

Neurons or Neutrons? The model picks one piece at a time
NeuronsNeuronswhat you asked about
NeutronsNeutronswhat v8 wrote

At the start of an answer, after N e, which piece comes next in the book?

utr379 timesNeutrons
ur32Neurons
  1. The model writes one piece at a time. "Neurons" and "Neutrons" both start with N then e, so after two pieces both words are still possible.
  2. For the third piece, the book has utr (Neutrons) after N e 379 times, and ur (Neurons) only 32 times.
  3. The model always takes the favourite. So it picks utr, and the word comes out as "Neutrons".

So the model leans towards the word it saw most often, when two words start with the same pieces. v7 fell into the same trap in a different way. It wrote "Neon", which also starts with N e.

A bigger model does not fully fix this. A bigger model can only remember what its book says, and how often it says it.

Advanced Task: Count The Shelf By Hand

The shelf (the feed-forward) in one layer has two parts. The first part turns 512 numbers into 2,048. The second part turns 2,048 back into 512. Each part also has one extra number for every output.

  1. Work out the numbers in one layer's shelf: (512 × 2,048 + 2,048) + (2,048 × 512 + 512).
  2. Multiply by 6 layers.
  3. Check that you get 12,598,272. That is the 12.6M in the Section 2 table.

Now do the same sum for v7 with 256 and 1,024. Divide the v8 answer by the v7 answer. Explain in one sentence why you get about 4.

⚙ Troubleshooting & FAQs

The parameter count prints 7951872

Your attention_engine.py still says n_embd = 256, or you ran the command in your Session 4 folder. Open the Session 5 file and check the number near the top.

RuntimeError: Error(s) in loading state_dict or size mismatch

You tried to open a 256-wide model (v6 or v7) in your 512-wide code. Run v7 from your Session 4 folder, and run v8 from your Session 5 folder.

v8 answers more slowly than v7

That is normal. v8 has 3.2 times as many numbers to work through for every token. On our laptop one answer took about 3 seconds with v8 and about 2 seconds with v7.

v7 got a question right that v8 got wrong

It happens. In our test, v7 gave the better answer on 35 questions. One question can go either way. That is why the 200-question test matters more than any single answer.
